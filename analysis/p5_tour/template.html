Faithful, or leaning on its prior?

One question, asked carefully. Two kinds of neural network learn to calibrate an emulated rough Heston model: do they give the exact Bayesian answer, or lean on their training more than that answer does? P5 · the foundation

How did the study run?

P5 measures networks against an exact reference. Before any network saw the test set, a chain of gates had to pass, and each one could stop the study in public. One version did stop. Walk the chain below.

the model

A frozen emulator

The pricer is too slow for this many runs, so G̃, interpolated from it with no training, replaces it. Networks and reference face the same G̃, so their gap, beyond Monte Carlo error, is the network's. lines 24, 48, 50, 54

the reference

Four long chains per surface

Each surface gets a Metropolis sampler: four chains, 10,000 burn-in and 40,000 sampling steps each, kept only if R-hat is at most 1.01 and the effective sample size of H is at least 2,000. line 68

the limit

Exact, for the emulator

It is exact up to Monte Carlo error, on synthetic surfaces with noise of a known form. It says nothing directly about real quotes or other models. lines 50, 150

P5 · stage 2 · H-b and H-c

Do they lean on their training
more than they should?

Some lean is correct. Bayes' rule says a prior should move the answer when the data are weak. So P5 trains each network twice, under prior A (mean H 0.12) and prior B (mean H 0.32), and shows both the same surface. The exact answer moves too. E is how much further the network moves than the exact answer does, in units of the 0.20 between the priors' means. lines 62, 63, 108, 109

01 · read a bar

Filled is A, hollow is B

Each row runs from the answer under prior A to the answer under prior B. A longer bar means the prior moved the answer further. The top row is what Bayes' rule requires.

02 · weaken the data

Drag toward low c

The exact bar grows as the data weaken. That growth is correct. The question is whether a network's bar is longer still.

P5 · H-d

Can you trust their
error bars?

A network also reports a 90% interval. m takes the networks trained under prior A, and is how much of the exact posterior under A falls inside that interval: a faithful interval scores 0.90. H-d bet that where the data say least, m falls below 0.80. P5 could not test this. This page shows what the test would have measured, and why it could not be run. lines 110, 124

01 · the exact bell

Faithful scores 0.90

The curve is an exact posterior and the band is a network's interval. With the right width and centre, 90% of the bell is inside.

02 · overconfidence

Narrow the interval

Drag the width down. At 0.78 of the exact width, m reaches H-d's 0.80. At 0.875 it reaches 0.85.

03 · off-centre

Slide the centre

The right width, half a posterior standard deviation off, scores 0.858. Being off-centre also costs mass.

Line 126's cases
Why H-d could not be run: the Flat bin against its floorline 101

A bin needs at least 10% of the accepted pairs and at least 100 pairs. H-d is scored in the Flat bin, and in every version of the analysis it falls short.

—
  • It was declared first. Stage 1 made this call from the references alone, before any network scored the test set, and nothing was adjusted to fill the bin. line 101; D106
  • It leaves each null unproven. The null needs H-d's interval inside [0.85, 0.95], so an untestable H-d alone keeps the ensemble's null unproven. line 128; D109
  • No median was taken. The cells file holds m at every pair, because Stage 2 computes it. No median of m in the Flat bin has been taken, and this page carries no m at all. D109
  • What stays unknown. The verdicts say nothing about whether either calibrator's 90% intervals are honest where the data are weakest.
The bell is the Gaussian reading in line 126, computed in your browser. The counts are from Stage 2's report (D109).
P5 · gate G3

How much training
is enough?

Gate G3 trained each network on 10,000 to 320,000 surfaces and checked two things on 16 pilot surfaces: that ξ₀ is recovered, and that the median |d| at a size stays within 0.002 of the median at twice that size. The training size is the largest that passes. line 144

A quiet ladder

02 · the ratio estimator

Prior A, 0.1 vol point

The median jumps around. Only the first rung is stable, so the rule takes 10,000, though the median there is above H-a's 0.03.

A reading, labelled as such (D109). G3 tests stability under a doubling, not the size of the error. So the rule can take a size where the error is still large and happens to hold steady for one step. For the ratio estimator under A at 0.1 vol point, it took 10,000 with a pilot median of 0.0338. On the test set the median of |d| at that level is 0.0677. P5 · stage 2 · the verdicts

What did the study conclude?

Four bets, made in advance, scored for two calibrators. Each testable row gives a median and its 95% interval, set against the bet. Confirmed needs the whole interval on the expected side. Refuted needs the whole interval beyond the refutation line. Anything else is Inconclusive. lines 119 to 124, 133

01 · the shaded zones

Two zones on each line

The violet zone is what the bet expected. The grey zone is where it counts as refuted. The space between them is the gap.

02 · the bar

Where the interval sits

The white bar is the 95% interval and the dot is the median. The small ticks are the five seeds' medians. The whole bar has to clear a line. The seed floor, on H-c, is how far two networks differ when only the training seed changes: the median abs(S) between seeds, the larger of the two priors' values. line 111

03 · a bet, not a grade

Refuted on H-b or H-c is not bad news

H-b bet on a lean. For the ensemble, Refuted means the median lies below the refutation line. A smaller lean remains. Move the cut-offs above the board to see what holds.

The null, per calibratorline 128

The null is the finding that a calibrator is faithful and its prior dependence is exactly Bayes' rule. It needs H-a Confirmed, H-b, H-c and H-d Refuted, and H-d's interval inside [0.85, 0.95]. It is read on the primary verdicts only.

Ensemble○ unproven
Its first three verdicts are what the null asks. H-d being untestable is what leaves it unproven.
Ratio estimator○ unproven
Its H-a and H-c are Inconclusive, and its H-b Confirmed is the opposite of the Refuted the null asks.

So line 128's finding, and the recommendation it would carry, are claimed for neither calibrator.
