## The question

Where a surface carries little information about H, the exact posterior returns the prior. That is Bayes' rule, not a defect, and v2 does not test it.

The question is the gap between a learned calibrator and that exact posterior:

> Does an amortised calibrator for rough Heston reproduce the exact posterior of H under its own training prior, or does it lean on the prior more than the exact posterior does, and does the gap grow as the surface loses information about H?

- **Q1 (model, no network).** On a stated grid, at stated noise levels, how much does the surface tell us about H across the parameter box?
- **Q2 (network).** Given Q1, how far is each calibrator's answer from the exact posterior's answer on the same surface?

## Objects

**Parameters.** H in [0.02, 0.48], ν in [0.05, 0.35], ρ in [−0.90, −0.70], ξ₀ in [0.04, 0.25]: the box on which G0's check passed (D62), taken by the criterion under Gates from the proposal ν [0.05, 1.00], ρ [−0.99, 0.00], ξ₀ [0.001, 0.25] of `layer4_calibrate_surface.py` (H upper bound lowered from 0.49). κ fixed at 0.30, per D38 EXP 4. *The box.* Narrowed by the order written before the run and not by hand: its ρ and ν edges are where the check's doubled-`U_max` solve stays finite within the ladder, its ξ₀ floor and the ρ end near −1 where truncation and the inversion fail (see "What was known before freezing"). The priors of H below and the 0.20 that separates their means are written for this range of H, which is unchanged. After freezing no gate changes the box.

**Grid.** v1 said "P1's grid"; the repository has three (D37 single smile, D38 synthetic surface, D39 live Deribit). v2 uses D38's: maturities {0.10, 0.25, 0.50, 1.00, 2.00}, seven standardised moneyness points {−2, −1, −0.5, 0, 0.5, 1, 2}, 35 quotes. *Five maturities.* G0's check passed on all five (D62): ten candidates passed on five maturities, so by the order under Gates the four-maturity grid was not taken. In the 3 October probe its ATM quote was finite at `N_riccati` 2000 in two of the four failing points followed up and still non-finite at 4000 in the other two; no result above 4000 is recorded for T = 2.00. D41 found the same symptom at the one-year tenor to be a limit of resolution, and the cost of the Riccati solve grows as the square of `N_riccati`. D41 found the one-year tenor, the longest it priced, the least informative about H on live BTC at the fitted optimum; what T = 2.00 adds about H has not been measured in this repository, and G0's pilot did not measure it. D38's figures above were measured with T = 2.00 in the grid, and D38 advised five or more maturities for a market run. After freezing no gate changes the grid.

**Observable.** D38 anchors strikes once from the target's noiseless ATM vol, which hands a Bayesian observer the exact ATM term structure. v2 closes that leak by the first of two options:

1. *Self-standardised (chosen).* The model is priced with spot S0 and a zero interest rate, as in `layer4_calibrate_surface.py`, so strike S0 is at the money. For maturity T and standardised moneyness point z of the grid above, the quote is the model implied vol at strike S0·exp(z·σ_ATM(θ, T)·√T), where σ_ATM(θ, T) is θ's own noiseless implied vol at strike S0 for that maturity. The forward map is G: θ → one number for each quote of that grid, ordered by maturity and then by z, both ascending. The reference and the networks take as input the noisy quotes in that order and nothing else that depends on θ; the strikes and the noiseless σ_ATM are withheld, and that is what closes the leak. The noisy quote at z = 0 is σ_ATM plus noise. Implied vols are decimals: one vol point is 0.01.
2. *Fixed absolute grid (not chosen).* Fixed log-moneyness and maturities, as in part of the deep-calibration literature, with ξ₀ narrowed so every quote is priceable. It would change the box, and no grid values or ξ₀ range were proposed. The chosen observable is further from those published networks that are trained on a fixed strike grid: no claim is made about quotes at fixed strikes, and D38's figures, measured with strikes anchored once on the target, do not carry over to the chosen observable as measured.

**The model is a frozen emulator.** G̃ approximates G over the box and is built once from the pricer. The calibrators' training data and the reference posterior both use G̃. Consequences, stated plainly:

- Q2 is exact: network and reference face the same model, so their gap is the network's alone.
- Q1 describes G̃. It is claimed for rough Heston only at noise levels where gate G1 shows the emulator error is small against s.
- The training surfaces are priced by the path of `p5_g0_main.py` at the resolution and inversion settings under Gates, three Riccati solves per (H, ν, ρ, T) shared across strikes and ξ₀; `surface_model` in `layer4_calibrate_surface.py`, which re-solves the Riccati per strike, is not used.

**Emulator form and design** (D64). G̃ is a tensor-product Chebyshev interpolant of G on the box, one interpolant per quote of the grid, in the four parameters (H, ν, ρ, ξ₀) mapped affinely from the box to [−1, 1]⁴, on Chebyshev nodes of the first kind, evaluated by the barycentric formula; it is deterministic, with no training and no seed, and exact at its nodes. Nodes: 16 in H, 8 in ν, 6 in ρ and 12 in ξ₀, so 9,216 training surfaces at 768 (H, ν, ρ) triples, the ξ₀ nodes sharing each triple's Riccati solves. Each training surface is priced at the resolution and inversion settings under Gates, its strikes anchored on its own ATM quote at that setting, as the observable above requires; a training quote that is not finite stops the build, the box having passed G0 on its pilot and confirming points. Held-out set: 200 points uniform on the box from numpy's `default_rng(73)`, priced the same way; the emulator error e of G1 is the 99th percentile, over their 7,000 quotes, of the absolute difference between G̃ and G, with the maximum and the per-maturity 99th percentiles reported beside it. The emulator is built after freezing, under G1, from a script committed with its output. Cost, from D62: 968 sets of Riccati solves at about 288 core-seconds each with the pool running, about 77 core-hours.

**Noise.** Independent Gaussian on each quote, as a design factor: s in {0.1, 0.3, 0.5} vol point (D65). They are the three levels at which H's scatter under noise has been measured in this repository (D38); read against the priors' standard deviations, D38's figures place one level in each bin as proposed, at D38's point, which lies on three of the box's faces (see "What was known before freezing"). 0.1 is P1's level. 1.0 vol point, of the order of the live misfit, is not kept: the live regime lies outside the box, and at that level, by the reading in D65, every pair would be flat. Noise level is how the design reaches the flat regime, by construction. Well-specified noise only; no claim about real quotes.

**Priors.** Same support; all other parameters uniform on the box.

**The exact reference** (D66). Per scored surface and noise level: the posterior of θ on the box under the likelihood Gaussian with the known s on the 35 quotes and the uniform box prior, by adaptive random-walk Metropolis on G̃ in the box mapped affinely to [0, 1]⁴. One generator per pair, `default_rng([83, i, j])` for the i-th test θ and the j-th noise level, serves the starts, the proposals and the acceptance draws. Four chains from points uniform on the box; the proposal Gaussian, its covariance 2.38²/4 times 1/12 times the identity at the start and, every 500 of the 10,000 burn-in steps per chain, reset to 2.38²/4 times the empirical covariance of that chain's steps so far, its diagonal floored at 1e-10; 40,000 sampling steps per chain with the proposal fixed; proposals outside the box rejected. Accepted when, on the sampling steps only and with each chain split into halves, the classical between-within split R-hat (not rank-normalised) is at most 1.01 on each parameter and the effective sample size of H over the eight half-chains, by the same between-within estimator, is at least 2,000; otherwise repeated once with both lengths doubled from fresh starts of the same generator, a second failure being a reference failure for that pair, declared. Posteriors under A and B by self-normalised importance weights proportional to the prior's density of H at each pooled sampling step, with Kish's effective sample size (Σw)²/Σw² at least 1,000 under each, else a direct run under that prior with the same sampler and acceptance; means, variances, quantiles and interval probabilities as weighted means and weighted quantiles of the pooled sampling steps. Reported per pair: the posterior means of H under A and B, its posterior variance under A, the equal-tailed 90% interval under A, the posterior probability under A of any given interval, the acceptance rate, R-hat and the effective sample sizes.

**The calibrators.** Each trained per prior and per noise level.

1. Deep ensemble of five heteroscedastic members, Gaussian negative log-likelihood. Ĥ is the ensemble mean; the 90% interval is the central Gaussian interval at the ensemble's total variance.
2. Neural ratio estimator. Ĥ is the posterior mean; the 90% interval is equal-tailed.

## Stratification

Each scored (surface, noise level) pair is binned by the exact posterior's contraction of H under prior A:

c = 1 − Var_post(H | y) / Var_prior(H)

| Bin | Contraction |
| --- | --- |
| Identified | c ≥ 0.9 |
| Partial | 0.5 ≤ c < 0.9 |
| Flat | c < 0.5 |

c depends only on the observed surface and the reference. Fisher information and CRB_H are reported as descriptive companions and define nothing. **OPEN — the cut-offs.**

## Stage 1 — the identifiability map (Q1)

From the reference alone: the distribution of c per noise level, the share of pairs in each bin, and c against true (H, ν).

Descriptive, with one stop rule: a bin holding fewer than 10% of pairs makes its hypotheses untestable, and they are declared so, not adjusted. **OPEN — the 10% floor.**

## Stage 2 — fidelity to the exact posterior (Q2)

Per pair, with subscripts for the training prior:

- **Mean error.** d = Ĥ_net,A − Ĥ_exact,A.
- **Shift ratio.** S = (Ĥ_B − Ĥ_A) / 0.20, for the network and for the reference.
- **Excess prior dependence.** E = S_net − S_exact. Positive means the network leans on its prior more than Bayes' rule requires.
- **Interval mass.** m = exact posterior probability, under prior A, of the network's 90% interval. Faithful is 0.90.
- **Seed floor.** S between two networks trained on the same prior with different seeds.

Every comparison is per pair against the reference, so nothing rests on frequentist coverage within strata.

## Hypotheses

Named H-a to H-d to avoid collision with papers P1–P4. Directions and numbers are the author's bets and are **OPEN**.

| | Measure | Bin | Expected | Refuted if |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **H-a Faithful when informed** | median of abs(d) | Identified | below 0.01 | above 0.03 |
| **H-b Excess prior dependence** | median E | Partial | above 0.15 | below 0.05 |
| **H-c Leak into the identified regime** | median S_net | Identified | above 0.10 and above the seed floor | below 0.05 |
| **H-d Interval infidelity** | median m | Flat | outside [0.80, 0.95] | inside [0.85, 0.93] |

In the flat bin the reference itself has S near one, so excess prior dependence is not testable there; only interval fidelity is. Each hypothesis is scored per calibrator, pooled over noise levels, with the per-level breakdown reported. No aggregate verdict.

**Null.** Both calibrators match the reference within tolerance in every bin. Then the finding is that amortised calibration is faithful here and its prior dependence is exactly Bayes' rule; the practical recommendation becomes "report the contraction next to the estimate".

## Analysis plan

- 95% hierarchical bootstrap over training seeds and, within seed, pairs.
- **Confirmed**: whole interval on the expected side of the expected value. **Refuted**: whole interval beyond the refutation threshold. **Inconclusive**: otherwise.
- Pre-specified dose-response: Spearman correlation of c with abs(d), with interval.
- Exploratory, labelled: fidelity against training-prior density at true H; which ensemble variance component tracks c; a two-step optimiser through G̃.

## Gates

Pilots. No pilot output enters the results. G0's pilot was run before freezing (D60 to D62) and G1 to G3 are run after freezing; G0's output fixed the box, the grid and the resolution, and is declared under "What was known before freezing".

1. **G0 — pricer over the box.** Existing verification tests pass: the repository's test suite, run on the author's machine. On pilot points across the box, including its corners, every quote is finite and changes by less than the tolerance in each comparison below. Cost is measured. G0 is run before freezing, as a pilot on the author's machine, from a script committed to the repository with its output; it is not the 3 October probe. *Tolerance.* 0.01 vol point (0.0001), absolute, on every quote and in each comparison separately. That is a tenth of the smallest noise level under Noise and half of what G1 allows the emulator at that level. *Comparisons.* Each quote is compared with itself under refinement of each numerical setting in turn: `N_riccati` doubled; the node count doubled; and `U_max` raised, both pricings at an `N_riccati` high enough for the quote at the raised `U_max` to stay finite. Doubling `N_riccati` alone does not show an error of the inversion: both pricings use the same nodes and the same `U_max`, so such an error is, to leading order, the same in both. The comparisons are checks of convergence, not a bound on the pricer's error. *What the pilot computes.* The existing tests; timing; whether each quote is finite and, if not, at which stage of the calculation it failed; the comparisons above; the inversion applied to characteristic functions for which a reference price is available (Black–Scholes and classical Heston); and the modulus of the characteristic function at the highest node, which is recorded and decides nothing. Beyond the existing tests it computes nothing that compares quotes across parameter values: no Jacobian, Fisher information, posterior, calibration or emulator. *Two runs.* A scoping run is made first, on the box and the grid proposed under Objects, its 16 corners included. It has no pass or fail: it measures cost on the author's machine and, at each point and maturity it prices, whether and from which `N_riccati` among those it tries the quotes are finite, and which comparisons are met. Its points and the values of `N_riccati`, node count and `U_max` it tries are those of the script as committed before it is run. The main run follows; its settings are written in here before it is run, with the scoping run's output in view, and are declared as such. *Cost limit.* A resolution is admissible on a grid if, and only if, the Riccati solves for one (H, ν, ρ) over that grid's maturities, at the node count from which the comparisons start, take at most 1,200 core-seconds, as the median over the points priced, on the author's machine with the pilot's worker pool running. That is two weeks of four cores for 4,000 such sets of solves; it presumes an emulator design of that order, which is not yet fixed (see Objects). *Criterion.* The box, the grid and the resolution are taken from the main run's output by the order that follows. The range of H is not narrowed. A candidate is a box whose bounds on ν, ρ and ξ₀ are values priced in the main run and whose corners are all pilot points. A candidate passes on a grid at a resolution if that resolution is admissible on that grid and, at every pilot point in the candidate, its corners included, every quote of that grid is finite and meets every comparison, there and at every finer resolution checked (the `U_max` comparison at the `N_riccati` used for it). The candidates considered are the passing ones that contain D38's point (H 0.10, ν 0.35, ρ −0.70, ξ₀ 0.04), or all passing ones if none contains it. If any of them passes on five maturities, the grid is the five and only those are kept; otherwise the grid is the four without T = 2.00. Of those kept, the one keeping the largest share of the proposed ranges of ν, ρ and ξ₀ (the product of the three fractions) is taken, at the coarsest resolution at which it passes on that grid. It is then confirmed on fresh uniform points inside it, tested as pilot points are; the fresh points count as pilot points from then on, and if the candidate then no longer passes on that grid at that resolution the order is applied again. If no candidate passes, the check has passed on no box and grid and the draft is not frozen as it stands; anything adopted after that is declared as a judgement made on seen output. Pilot seeds are stated with each run; none is 20261002, the sealed seed, or 7, the probe's. *The main run's settings*, written in with the scoping run's output in view (D61) and before the main run was made; the script is `p5_g0_main.py` as committed. Resolutions: one `N_riccati` per maturity, from the ladder 2000, 4000, 8000 climbed per point and maturity, with a base solve at 16000 made only for the `N_riccati` comparison at 8000 and only where the base solve at 8000 is finite (16000 is not a resolution: it is tried as the partner alone, on cost, D61); a resolution is the vector of per-maturity values, and a candidate's resolution is the per-maturity smallest at which it passes, a point whose task stopped at a coarser rung by the stop rule counting as passing at every finer `N_riccati`. Inversion: `U_max` = 250/√T, rounded to 790, 500, 350, 250 and 180 at T = 0.10, 0.25, 0.50, 1.00 and 2.00, with 504, 320, 224, 160 and 112 nodes (the scoping run's spacing, to the nearest multiple of 8); the node count doubled at the same `U_max`; `U_max` doubled with the node count doubled, compared with the doubled-node setting; the `U_max` comparison made at every rung at the rung's own `N_riccati`. 2. **G1 — emulator.** On held-out pricer points, the 99th-percentile absolute implied-vol error e is reported. Q1 is claimed for rough Heston only at noise levels with e ≤ s / 5. The ratio is 5 (D64): at s = 0.1 that is 0.02 vol point, the basis of the pricer's tolerance under G0. If e exceeds s / 5 at the smallest noise level under Noise (0.1), the node count is doubled once, in the dimension whose Chebyshev coefficient tail is largest (the tail of a dimension being the largest absolute Chebyshev coefficient, over all 35 quotes and all indices in the other three dimensions, at that dimension's highest index), and the build repeated at the new nodes, declared as such; there is no second refinement, and Q1 is then claimed at the noise levels this gate allows and at no other.
3. **G2 — reference.** On 12 pilot surfaces (4 points uniform on the box, `default_rng(79)`, at the three noise levels, one noise draw each, `default_rng(89)`; none a test surface) the MCMC posterior of H matches a brute-force grid posterior on G̃ by quadrature: a cell-centred tensor grid of 32 points per dimension over the smallest axis-aligned box containing the four chains' sampling-phase samples, each side extended by 20% and clipped to the box, the posterior being the prior-A-weighted likelihood summed over cells; and, at the largest noise level, as a check against a mode the chains missed, a second grid of 48 cell-centred points in each of H, ν and ρ over the whole box with ξ₀ on the 32 points of the first grid; tolerance, under prior A on every pilot surface: the posterior mean of H within 0.001 and the contraction c within 0.01 (D66). Cost per posterior is measured and fixes the test-set size.
4. **G3 — training adequacy.** Per calibrator, prior and noise level, on 48 pilot surfaces (16 points uniform on the box, `default_rng(97)`, at the three noise levels, one noise draw each, `default_rng(101)`; none a test surface; the exact reference computed on each as under The exact reference): ξ₀ recovered with slope above 0.95, the slope of the least-squares line of the calibrator's point estimate of ξ₀ on the true ξ₀ over the pilot surfaces (each calibrator estimates ξ₀ as well as H); and abs(d), with d under the calibrator's own training prior, stable to a doubling of the training set: its median over the pilot surfaces at 2n within 0.002 of that at n, a fifth of H-a's expected value. Training sets of 10,000 × 2^k surfaces, k = 0 to 4, nested, with the first training seed; the training-set size is the smallest n at which both hold, the run at 2n made for the check only. Budget: 320,000 surfaces and 4 h of wall per training run on the author's machine. If none within budget passes, P5 stops; architecture is not tuned against H outputs (D67).
