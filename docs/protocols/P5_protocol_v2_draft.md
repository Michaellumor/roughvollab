## The question

Where a surface carries little information about H, the exact posterior returns the prior. That is Bayes' rule, not a defect, and v2 does not test it.

The question is the gap between a learned calibrator and that exact posterior:

> Does an amortised calibrator for rough Heston reproduce the exact posterior of H under its own training prior, or does it lean on the prior more than the exact posterior does, and does the gap grow as the surface loses information about H?

- **Q1 (model, no network).** On a stated grid, at stated noise levels, how much does the surface tell us about H across the parameter box?
- **Q2 (network).** Given Q1, how far is each calibrator's answer from the exact posterior's answer on the same surface?

**Observable.** D38 anchors strikes once from the target's noiseless ATM vol, which hands a Bayesian observer the exact ATM term structure. v2 closes that leak by the first of two options:

1. *Self-standardised (chosen).* The model is priced with spot S0 and a zero interest rate, as in `layer4_calibrate_surface.py`, so strike S0 is at the money. For maturity T and standardised moneyness point z of the grid above, the quote is the model implied vol at strike S0·exp(z·σ_ATM(θ, T)·√T), where σ_ATM(θ, T) is θ's own noiseless implied vol at strike S0 for that maturity. The forward map is G: θ → one number for each quote of that grid, ordered by maturity and then by z, both ascending. The reference and the networks take as input the noisy quotes in that order and nothing else that depends on θ; the strikes and the noiseless σ_ATM are withheld, and that is what closes the leak. The noisy quote at z = 0 is σ_ATM plus noise. Implied vols are decimals: one vol point is 0.01.
2. *Fixed absolute grid (not chosen).* Fixed log-moneyness and maturities, as in part of the deep-calibration literature, with ξ₀ narrowed so every quote is priceable. It would change the box, and no grid values or ξ₀ range were proposed. The chosen observable is further from those published networks that are trained on a fixed strike grid: no claim is made about quotes at fixed strikes, and D38's figures, measured with strikes anchored once on the target, do not carry over to the chosen observable as measured.

**The model is a frozen emulator.** G̃ approximates G over the box and is built once from the pricer. The calibrators' training data and the reference posterior both use G̃. Consequences, stated plainly:

- Q2 is exact up to the Monte Carlo error of the reference and, for the ratio estimator, of its own sampler: network and reference face the same model, so their gap is otherwise the network's alone (D81).
- Q1 describes G̃. It is claimed for rough Heston only at noise levels where gate G1 shows the emulator error is small against s.
- The training surfaces are priced by the path of `p5_g0_main.py` at the resolution and inversion settings under Gates, three Riccati solves per (H, ν, ρ, T) shared across strikes and ξ₀; `surface_model` in `layer4_calibrate_surface.py`, which re-solves the Riccati per strike, is not used.

**Emulator form and design** (D64). G̃ is a tensor-product Chebyshev interpolant of G on the box, one interpolant per quote of the grid, in the four parameters (H, ν, ρ, ξ₀) mapped affinely from the box to [−1, 1]⁴, on Chebyshev nodes of the first kind, evaluated by the barycentric formula; it is deterministic, with no training and no seed, and exact at its nodes. Nodes: 16 in H, 8 in ν, 6 in ρ and 12 in ξ₀, so 9,216 training surfaces at 768 (H, ν, ρ) triples, the ξ₀ nodes sharing each triple's Riccati solves. Each training surface is priced at the resolution and inversion settings under Gates, its strikes anchored on its own ATM quote at that setting, as the observable above requires, the box having passed G0 on its pilot and confirming points; a training or held-out point any of whose quotes is not finite is priced once more at `N_riccati` 16000 with the same inversion settings, its quotes then taken from that pricing (a training point being one surface, the other surfaces of its triple keep theirs), and if any quote is still not finite the build stops and P5 stops (D81). Held-out set: 200 points uniform on the box from numpy's `default_rng(73)`, priced the same way; the emulator error e of G1 is the 99th percentile, over their 7,000 quotes, of the absolute difference between G̃ and G, with the maximum and the per-maturity 99th percentiles reported beside it. The emulator is built after freezing, under G1, from a script committed with its output. Cost, from D62: 968 sets of Riccati solves at about 288 core-seconds each with the pool running, about 77 core-hours.

**Noise.** Independent Gaussian on each quote, as a design factor: s in {0.1, 0.3, 0.5} vol point (D65). They are the three levels at which H's scatter under noise has been measured in this repository (D38); read against the priors' standard deviations, D38's figures place one level in each bin as proposed, at D38's point, which lies on three of the box's faces (see "What was known before freezing"). 0.1 is P1's level. 1.0 vol point, of the order of the live misfit, is not kept: the live regime lies outside the box, and at that level, by the reading in D65, every pair would be flat. Noise level is how the design reaches the flat regime, by construction. Well-specified noise only; no claim about real quotes.

**The Beta parameters** (D69). Prior A is accepted as proposed. Prior B keeps v1's mean of 0.32 and takes prior A's variance, so the two priors differ only in where they put their mass, as v1 registered: on the unit interval B has mean 15/23 and variance 36/2645, A's, which fixes a + b = 47/3 exactly, a = 235/23 and b = 376/69. What they are, by arithmetic on the Beta density: A has its mode at H = 0.093, its central 90% on [0.046, 0.220], 2.0% of its mass at H ≥ 0.25, 0.1% at H ≥ 0.32 and 2.8e-6 above 0.40, so a calibrator trained under A in effect never sees the top sixth of the range; B has its mode at 0.330, its central 90% on [0.227, 0.403], 10.6% of its mass below 0.25 and 1.1e-4 at or below 0.12; the densities cross at H = 0.2197. The two are equal in variance; their skews differ (0.72 and −0.30), as any scaled-Beta pair with these means on this support must. The gap of 0.20 between the means is 3.73 standard deviations of either prior. Under a Gaussian approximation to the likelihood in H, equal prior variances make the exact reference's own shift ratio S equal to 1 − c, a function of the contraction alone, so at the edge of the Identified bin it is 0.10 wherever the data sit; with the proposed unequal variances (B's standard deviation 0.062) it ran from 0.065 to 0.118 at c = 0.9 as the likelihood's centre moves across the range of H, by the same approximation. Half of the uniform test θ lie where A has 2% of its mass, about 3,150 of an A-trained network's training surfaces at the largest training size, thinning to almost none towards the top of the range: about 15 with H above 0.36 under A, and about 18 with H below 0.12 under B, the two regions holding 48% of the test θ (by arithmetic on the priors, D81). Verdicts on the full test set therefore include extrapolation outside the training support as well as any lean on the prior; the secondary analysis under Analysis plan, on the pairs both priors cover, limits the extrapolation (D81). The test set is neutral so that neither calibrator has home advantage, and the prior swap asks what a calibrator does where its prior and the data disagree. A defines the bins and m because one prior must, for c to be one number per pair, and v1 made A the home prior (its control at home is on test H from prior A). Var_prior(H) under Stratification is the analytic variance of A, 0.46² × 36/2645 = 0.00288 exactly, not an estimate. Not taken: the proposed Beta(7.5, 4); a mirror pair, which would move a registered mean; a wider or narrower common width, A's 0.054 being the figure line 24 and the bins use; a perturbation of one prior in place of the swap, which would replace the registered design; training once on the uniform prior and reweighting, which is not the draft's question.

**The exact reference** (D66). Per scored surface and noise level: the posterior of θ on the box under the likelihood Gaussian with the known s on the 35 quotes and the uniform box prior, by adaptive random-walk Metropolis on G̃ in the box mapped affinely to [0, 1]⁴. One generator per pair, `default_rng([83, i, j])` for the i-th θ of the set in question (test or pilot) and the j-th noise level, i and j counted from 0 (j = 0, 1, 2 for 0.1, 0.3, 0.5; D81), serves the starts, the proposals and the acceptance draws; G2's retry is a set of its own, its i counted within it, so that its chains at the two fixed points start where the first attempt's did and draw the same proposals, only the noise differing (D82). Four chains from points uniform on the box; the proposal Gaussian, its covariance 2.38²/4 times 1/12 times the identity at the start and, every 500 of the 10,000 burn-in steps per chain, reset to 2.38²/4 times the empirical covariance of that chain's steps so far, its diagonal floored at 1e-10; 40,000 sampling steps per chain with the proposal fixed (both lengths doubled for every later run if G2's retry is needed, under Gates; D81); proposals outside the box rejected. Posteriors under A and B by self-normalised importance weights proportional to the prior's density of H at each pooled sampling step, accepted under a prior when the weighted chain's effective size, (Kish's (Σw)²/Σw² over the pooled sampling steps, divided by their number) × the effective sample size of H defined above, is at least 1,000 (D81), else a direct run under that prior with the same sampler and acceptance, a direct run that fails after its repeat making the pair a reference failure (D81); means, variances, quantiles and interval probabilities as weighted means and weighted quantiles of the pooled sampling steps. **The calibrators.** Each trained per prior and per noise level.

1. Deep ensemble of five heteroscedastic members, Gaussian negative log-likelihood. Ĥ is the ensemble mean; the 90% interval is the central Gaussian interval at the ensemble's total variance.
2. Neural ratio estimator. Ĥ is the posterior mean; the 90% interval is equal-tailed.

**Architecture and training** (D68). Both calibrators share one trunk: a multilayer perceptron with three hidden layers of 128 units and ReLU activations, no normalisation layers, no dropout, PyTorch's default initialisation, declared without search. Inputs: the 35 noisy quotes in the order of the observable, each standardised by its mean and standard deviation over the run's n training surfaces, fixed after training and applied to the validation, pilot and test surfaces; θ, where it is an input or a target, is mapped affinely from the box to [0, 1]⁴ as under The exact reference. *The ensemble.* Each member has four outputs: the means of H and ξ₀ in mapped coordinates and two raw variance terms, the variance being softplus(raw) + 1e-6; its loss is the sum over the two parameters of the Gaussian negative log-likelihood, averaged over the batch. Five members, each trained on the whole training set and differing by initialisation and shuffling; no adversarial step. Ĥ and the ξ₀ estimate are the means over members, mapped back to the box's units; the total variance of H is the mean over members of their variances of H plus the variance over members of their means of H (divisor 5, the variance of the equally weighted mixture of the members; D81), both in the box's units (the mapped variance times 0.46²), the two components reported separately; the 90% interval of item 1 above is Ĥ ± 1.645 times the square root of the total variance; nothing is clipped to the box. *The ratio estimator.* The binary-classifier variant: inputs the 35 standardised quotes and the mapped θ, 39 in all; one output, the logit, read as log r(θ, y). Each batch of B joint pairs (θᵢ, yᵢ), labelled 1, is paired with the B marginal pairs (θ_π(i), yᵢ), π the cyclic shift by one within the batch, labelled 0; the loss is binary cross-entropy over the 2B pairs. Its posterior under the training prior P has density proportional to P(H) · r(θ, y) on the box (P is uniform in ν, ρ and ξ₀). The ratio the classifier learns at its optimum, p(y | θ) / p_P(y), depends on the training prior only through a factor in y, so an exact ratio gives the exact posterior under P: E = 0 and m = 0.90, up to Monte Carlo error. Any excess dependence on its prior that the ratio estimator shows beyond Monte Carlo error is therefore error in the learned ratio. That error is expected to be largest where P puts little training mass, in the extrapolation that the secondary analysis on well-covered pairs limits (D84). The ratio estimator's posterior is sampled by the sampler of The exact reference with log P(H) + logit as the log-target: the same four chains, lengths (doubled with the reference's if G2's retry is needed; D81), adaptation, acceptance criteria and single repeat, with the generator `default_rng([107, p, q, i, j])` for prior p, training seed q, the i-th θ of the set in question counted from 0 and the j-th noise level counted from 1 as defined below (D81). Ĥ and the ξ₀ estimate are the means of H and ξ₀ over the pooled sampling steps of the accepted run, the 90% interval their equal-tailed quantiles; the posterior sample count is that run's pooled steps, 160,000 (4 × 40,000) or 320,000 after the repeat, at the lengths of The exact reference (twice those if G2's retry has doubled them). A sampler failure after the repeat is the calibrator's failure on that pair at that seed: the pair is excluded, at that seed, from every measure that uses the failed network's output, S and E at that seed included, and kept in measures that use only the other prior's network; the failures are counted and reported per bin beside the measures; a bin in which a calibrator's failed pairs (a pair on which any of its networks, at any seed and under either prior, failed; D81) exceed 10% of that bin's pairs, or leave fewer than 100, makes its hypotheses untestable for that calibrator, the floor of Stage 1 applied again per calibrator. *Optimiser and stopping.* Adam at learning rate 0.001 with β = (0.9, 0.999), no weight decay, batch 256 with a partial final batch kept, the learning rate constant: the stopping rule ends training, not a schedule. A validation set of 4,000 surfaces per prior and noise level, θ and noise from `default_rng([113, p, j])` under the training prior and noise level, shared by both calibrators, every seed and every rung of G3, and never a training, pilot or test surface; each calibrator's training objective is evaluated on it after every epoch, in its drawn order in batches of 256 with the final batch of 160 kept and the marginal pairs formed as in training, an epoch being one shuffled pass over the n training surfaces, whose quote noise is drawn once per surface and not afresh per epoch; training stops when that validation loss has not improved for 50 consecutive epochs or at 1,000 epochs, and the parameters at the best validation loss are kept; the rule acts on each member of the ensemble and on the one ratio estimator. Seed q fixes the training draw, θ and noise from `default_rng([109, p, j, q])`: one generator draws, in this order, the 320,000 values of H from the training prior, then the 320,000 triples (ν, ρ, ξ₀) uniform on the box, then the 320,000 × 35 standard normals ε, every seed drawing the whole block and a run at n taking the first n rows of each (D74, D81); G3's single draw of 320,000 is the case q = 1; and torch's generator, seeded with 109,000,000 + 10,000·p + 1,000·j + 10·q + m, where p is 1 for prior A and 2 for prior B, j is 1, 2, 3 for the noise levels 0.1, 0.3, 0.5, and m is the member, 1 to 5, or 0 for the ratio estimator. ## Stratification

Each scored (surface, noise level) pair is binned by the exact posterior's contraction of H under prior A:

c = 1 − Var_post(H | y) / Var_prior(H)

| Bin | Contraction |
| --- | --- |
| Identified | c ≥ 0.9 |
| Partial | 0.5 ≤ c < 0.9 |
| Flat | c < 0.5 |

c depends on the observed surface, prior A and the reference, and on no network. **The cut-offs** (D70). Accepted as proposed, 0.9 and 0.5. What they mean: under prior A (standard deviation 0.054) c ≥ 0.9 is a posterior standard deviation of H of at most 0.017 and c ≥ 0.5 of at most 0.038 (0.012 at 0.95, 0.027 at 0.75, 0.046 at 0.25). On a Gaussian reading of the likelihood in H, c = 0.5 is where the data and prior A carry equal precision about H, so the Flat bin is where the prior outweighs the data, and c = 0.9 is where the data carry nine times the prior's precision; 0.5 is the one value with a meaning of its own on that reading, and 0.9 is a round convention, a posterior variance a tenth of the prior's, kept as proposed and tested by the re-scoring below. On that reading, with the priors' variances equal (see The Beta parameters), the exact reference's own shift ratio is 1 − c: 0.10 at the Identified edge and 0.50 at the Flat edge, running towards one as c falls to zero. D65's reading of D38's figures places the three noise levels one per bin under these cut-offs (c about 0.97, 0.71 and 0.21 at D38's point). Of the sources the decision sheet read at source, Betancourt, Schad, Betancourt and Vasishth, and BayesFlow's diagnostic define contraction by this variance ratio and propose no formal cut-offs; the second describes, in a worked example, contraction below 0.5 as weak and values approaching one as very strong. *Conventions.* c is computed per pair from the pooled sampling steps of the reference's accepted run under prior A (the importance-weighted steps of the uniform-prior run, or the direct run under A where The exact reference requires one), as the weighted variance Σw(H − H̄)²/Σw with H̄ the weighted mean, against the analytic Var_prior(H) of The Beta parameters; it is not clipped, a negative c files under Flat by the table's inequality, and the number of such pairs is reported. Each pair is binned once, by its c as computed; the number of pairs within 0.02 of each cut-off is reported per bin, 0.02 being a round figure above G2's absolute tolerance on c and about the Monte Carlo error at the lower edge: by (1 − c)·√(2/(n − 1)) for a roughly Gaussian posterior, that error is about 0.004 at c = 0.9 and 0.022 at c = 0.5 at the 1,000 effective draws under A that The exact reference requires at least, and 0.003 and 0.016 at 2,000, the figure where the run under A is direct; G2 checks c to 0.01 on its pilot surfaces, or to four Monte Carlo standard errors where that is larger (D81): 0.089 at c = 0.5 and 0.018 at c = 0.9 at 1,000 effective draws (Gates; D82). Each hypothesis is also re-scored twice, with both cut-offs moved together by +0.05 (0.95 and 0.55) and by −0.05 (0.85 and 0.45), the floor under Stage 1 applied to each shifted bin, reported beside the verdict and deciding nothing. Not taken: equal-occupancy bins, v1's quintiles, because the hypotheses' numbers refer to a fixed meaning of each bin and the floor under Stage 1 handles occupancy; two bins, which would remove the bin H-b is scored in; hypotheses on c as a continuous variable, which the dose-response under Analysis plan already reports for abs(d); a divergence-based contraction, a different quantity.

## Stage 1 — the identifiability map (Q1)

From the reference alone: the distribution of c per noise level, the share of pairs in each bin, and c against true (H, ν); and the coverage covariate, per pair and per prior P: the mass prior P puts on the equal-tailed 90% interval of H under the exact posterior under P, a pair being well covered when both masses are at least 0.001, with the share of pairs well covered reported per bin (D81).

Descriptive, with one stop rule (D70): a bin holding fewer than 10% of the test pairs on which the reference was accepted, pooled over the noise levels (a reference failure under The exact reference removes its pair from the count and is reported), or fewer than 100 such pairs, makes its hypotheses untestable for both calibrators; that is declared from the reference alone, before any network scores the test set, by a run of Stage 1 alone, from the references, whose output the scoring of the test set and Stage 2 require (D81), and untestable is a fourth outcome beside the three verdicts under Analysis plan. Nothing is adjusted to fill a bin: the cut-offs, the noise levels and the number of test θ stand as written, and bin shares seen on G2's or G3's pilot surfaces decide nothing. The share is of the pooled pairs because the verdicts are pooled over noise levels; the count of 100 is chosen, not derived, as a floor for the bootstrap of a median that a share alone cannot give (at N = 400 test θ, 1,200 pairs, the 10% is 120 pairs and governs; the count would govern below N = 334). The floor counts pairs because the verdicts and the bootstrap under Analysis plan are over pairs and seeds; 100 pairs may be as few as 34 θ at three pairs per θ, and the number of distinct θ in each bin is reported beside the count. A share near 10% is itself uncertain, by about ±1.7 points on 1,200 pairs treated as independent, which the three pairs sharing a θ are not; the rule applies to the observed share. Reference failures are reported by noise level and by true (H, ν); if at any noise level they exceed 10% of that level's test pairs (more than 40 of its 400), P5 stops at Stage 1 (D81).

## Stage 2 — fidelity to the exact posterior (Q2)

Per pair, with subscripts for the training prior:

- **Mean error.** d_P = Ĥ_net,P − Ĥ_exact,P for the training prior P, where Ĥ_net,P is the calibrator's point estimate (under The calibrators) and Ĥ_exact,P the exact posterior mean of H under P (under The exact reference); d alone is d_A.
- **Shift ratio.** S = (Ĥ_B − Ĥ_A) / 0.20, for the network and for the reference.
- **Excess prior dependence.** E = S_net − S_exact, the networks trained under A and under B at the same training seed paired (under The calibrators); by the definitions above E = (d_B − d_A) / 0.20 on every pair. Positive means the network leans on its prior more than Bayes' rule requires.
- **Interval mass.** m = exact posterior probability, under prior A, of the 90% interval of the network trained under A, at each seed (D81). Faithful is 0.90.
- **Seed floor.** For each prior, the median, over the bin's pairs and the ten unordered pairs of training seeds, of abs(S) between the two networks trained on that prior with those seeds; the floor is the larger of the two priors' values. A hypothesis's interval is "above the seed floor" when it lies wholly above it. For scale: two networks differing by independent noise of standard deviation τ in H give a median abs(S) of 0.954 τ / 0.20, which is 0.0095 at τ = 0.002, G3's stability tolerance taken as a scale. The floor is a fixed number per bin, not bootstrapped, recomputed on each shifted bin of the re-scoring under Stratification; a seed pair in which either network failed on a pair (under The calibrators) is left out for that pair.

Every comparison is per pair against the reference, so nothing rests on frequentist coverage within strata.

## Hypotheses

Named H-a to H-d to avoid collision with papers P1–P4. Directions and numbers are the author's bets (D71); why each sits where it does is under the table.

| | Measure | Bin | Expected | Refuted if |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **H-a Faithful when informed** | median of abs(d) | Identified | below 0.01 | above 0.03 |
| **H-b Excess prior dependence** | median E | Partial | above 0.15 | below 0.05 |
| **H-c Leak into the identified regime** | median E | Identified | above 0.05 and above the seed floor | below 0.02 |
| **H-d Interval infidelity** | median m | Flat | below 0.80 | above 0.85 |

In the flat bin the reference itself has a large S, above 0.5 on the Gaussian reading under The cut-offs and approaching one as c falls to zero, so excess prior dependence is not testable there; only interval fidelity is. Each hypothesis is scored per calibrator, pooled over noise levels, a training-seed index q being one unit across the three levels; the per-level breakdown is reported and carries no verdict. The eight verdicts are read individually at 95% without adjustment for multiplicity, and the report says so. No verdict is pooled across hypotheses or calibrators; the null under Null is read off the four verdicts and is not a fifth verdict. A result inside the gap between Expected and Refuted is reported as Inconclusive, not stretched into a confirmation; the numbers are falsification thresholds, not targets, and no code may be tuned to reach them. *Why each number sits where it does*, a reading labelled as such. H-a: 0.01 is 0.59 of the largest posterior standard deviation the Identified bin allows (0.017, under The cut-offs) and 0.19 of prior A's; a faithful calibrator's d carries the reference's sampler error, about 0.0005 per pair (D68, from the decision sheet's arithmetic: a posterior standard deviation of 0.017 over the square root of 1,000 effective draws), and seed-to-seed scatter, for which G3's stability tolerance of 0.002 is taken as the scale (G3 bounds a median's shift under a doubling of the training set, not that scatter); these are per-pair scales, not the error of a median, and "below 0.01" leaves five times the larger of them; 0.03 is 1.77 posterior standard deviations at the bin's edge, and an estimate that far off where H is identified is not faithful; the 0.01 is also what G2's and G3's tolerances are built on. H-b: through the identity E = (d_B − d_A) / 0.20, E = 0.05 is a difference of 0.010 in H between the two networks' mean errors, H-a's fidelity scale, and E = 0.15 is 0.030, H-a's refutation scale; on the Gaussian reading a network that returns its prior mean scores E = c, 0.5 to 0.9 across the Partial bin, so 0.15 is a modest leak, and a network within 0.01 of the reference under both priors cannot exceed 0.10 on any pair. H-c: scored on E, not on S_net, so that it compares with the reference as every other row does and tests the network's own contribution; on the Gaussian reading the reference's own shift ratio S_exact is 1 − c, 0.10 at the Identified edge, so a bet on S_net above 0.10 would have been met by Bayes' rule alone; 0.05 is 0.010 in H, H-a's scale, where the data carry at least nine times the prior's precision; 0.02 is 0.004 in H, the scale of the per-pair sampler error of the quantities in E (0.005 in E for two references), about what can be told from zero on a pair; the seed floor is a second floor for the same reason. The gap from 0.02 to 0.05 is narrow against the seed-noise scale of 0.0095, so Inconclusive is a likely outcome for H-c and is reported as such. The direction of H-b and H-c is this reading: the test θ are uniform on the box while each network trains under A or B, so on about half the test range one network is far from its training mass (about 46% of test H lies above A's 99th percentile and 36% below B's 1st, which is at H = 0.1875; the 32% the decision sheet computed was for the earlier B, D81); a source the sheet read reports that amortised posterior errors grow with the distance between the data and the typical set of the training simulations; and a network pulled towards its own prior mean gives E > 0 whether the surface sits at low H (d_B > 0) or at high H (d_A < 0). So H-c is in effect the bet that the B-trained network is not faithful at H-a's scale where H is identified, and H-b the bet that the pull grows as the data weaken. The same reading is a limitation: on those pairs a network's training surfaces near the truth thin out with the distance from its prior's mass, to almost none at the far end (at the largest training size, within 0.034 of the truth, twice the Identified edge's posterior standard deviation: about 1,700 at H = 0.30 and 10 at 0.40 under A, about 1,300 at 0.15 and 60 at 0.10 under B; by arithmetic on the priors, D81), so a positive E there may come from extrapolation outside the training support rather than from leaning on the prior, and verdicts on the full test set cannot tell the two apart; the secondary analysis under Analysis plan, on the pairs both priors cover, limits the extrapolation (D81). H-d: one-sided towards overconfidence, the failure Hermans and co-authors report for amortised ratio estimation (the source the decision sheet read), and the one that matters to a user of the interval; m = 0.80 is a network interval 0.78 times the exact width (Gaussian posterior, same centre) and 0.85 is 0.875 times, while a centre off by half a posterior standard deviation with the right width scores 0.858. An interval wholly above 0.95 is Refuted on this row, which bets on overconfidence only; the share of pairs with m above 0.95 is reported beside it and carries no verdict, and the null under Null excludes it. For the ensemble, the central Gaussian interval of a skewed posterior scores 0.9225 in the limit where the posterior is prior A with the right mean and variance, so H-d is an easier Refuted for the ensemble than for the ratio estimator; that asymmetry works against H-d for the ensemble and is declared here.

**Null.** For a calibrator, the null stands when H-a is Confirmed, H-b, H-c and H-d are Refuted, and H-d's interval lies wholly within [0.85, 0.95] (D81; as first written it asked all four to be Refuted, which a faithful calibrator, scoring H-a Confirmed, could never meet); an Inconclusive or untestable outcome anywhere leaves it unproven, it is stated per calibrator, and it is read on the primary verdicts only, not on the secondary analysis under Analysis plan. Then the finding is that amortised calibration is faithful here and its prior dependence is exactly Bayes' rule; the practical recommendation becomes "report the contraction next to the estimate".

## Relation to prior work

Drafted with AI assistance (Claude) from the sources listed at the end of this section, each read at the address given on 11 October 2026 (D76), and not from the author's own reading, which the placeholder this section replaces named as its condition; statements are paraphrases with a pinpoint, and no third-party text is quoted. Where a source read here does not bear out a sentence of v1, the section says so and labels it as a reading.

**Deep calibration of rough volatility.** Three routes, recorded in v1 and re-read here where an arXiv version exists. Hernandez (2016) maps quotes straight to parameters with a feed-forward network and attaches no uncertainty; that is v1's record, which declares it checked against the paper, and it was not re-read for this section (the SSRN page refused the request, which was not worked around). Horvath, Muguruza and Tomas (2021 per v1; the arXiv version of 2019 is what was read, the journal version not having been reached) learn the map from parameters to an implied-volatility grid offline and calibrate through it with a classical optimiser, advocating the two-step route over Hernandez's direct calibration on the grounds of speed at the time of use and of keeping existing model knowledge and libraries valid (Sections 2.2 and 2.4); success is judged by the time per surface evaluation (Table 2), by relative error in implied-volatility space against Monte Carlo confidence bands (Section 4.1) and, for calibration, by parameter error and the fitted surface's root-mean-square error on synthetic parameters (Section 4.2), so v1's "price-space error" is not what those sections report, Section 4.3 on barrier options not having been read; no uncertainty is attached to the calibrated parameters, and a Bayesian analysis is deferred to separate work (Section 2). Bayer and Stemper (2018) pair a Levenberg–Marquardt routine with a network trained on the implied-volatility map of rough stochastic volatility models, rough Bergomi as the example (abstract). Bayer, Horvath, Muguruza, Stemper and Tomas (2019) consolidate that line and add a Bayesian analysis: a liquidity-weighted non-linear Bayes regression through the pointwise surrogate, sampled by MCMC, for rough Bergomi with four parameters (Sections 4.2.1 and 5.3). The prior is written as a density and no distribution is given for it in the text; the uniform ranges of Section 5.1 and Appendix A are the sampling ranges of the training data, and v1's "uniform prior on a truncated box" is a reading of those ranges. On a synthetic surface the text describes unimodal posteriors peaking close to or at the true parameters (Figure 6), and on SPX peaks at or close to values previously reported (Figure 7); the values of H v1 reads off those figures are not stated in the text and were not checked here, the figures being images that were not read. Section 5.3 adds that on the synthetic surface an increase or decrease in one of η, H and ρ can be offset by the others with no effect on implied volatility, so that the posterior cannot tell those configurations apart, which the off-diagonal plots for (η, H) and (η, ρ) show as diagonal elliptical level sets, and that on SPX this cannot at first glance be confirmed in the off-diagonal plots, though the scatter plots show remnants of it. Appendix A reports that a network trained on the inverse map, from surfaces to parameters, does not generalise properly out of sample, on surfaces that rough Bergomi did not generate; that such a one-step route cannot by design be trained on every market scenario, and so does not generalise to every setting; and that, the inverse map being poorly understood, the route is harder to justify for risk management (both passages re-read at the same address on 23 October 2026; D84). No posterior under a second prior appears (Sections 3.2.1 and 4.2.1; Remark 3 concerns the distribution of the training data), and no Fisher information or Cramér–Rao bound; a Jacobian of the pricing map is defined for the Levenberg–Marquardt step and its evaluation timed (Section 2, Remark 2, Table 1), so v1's "none of the four papers computes a Jacobian" holds on this reading only as none computing one to ask what the surface identifies. The arXiv page shows one version and no journal reference, so v1's "published 2025" is not re-verified here. Brigo, Huser and Leonte (2026) take the Bayesian route on the rough Heston model itself: telescoping ratio estimation, one binary classifier per conditional parameter on a shared encoder (Section 3.1; Appendix E.2), learns the posterior of (κ, θ, ν, ρ, V₀, H) under a uniform simulation prior on a box with H in (0.05, 0.45) and ρ in (−0.95, −0.10) (Section 5.1.1, Table 1), from a surface of nine standardised-moneyness points at seven maturities from five trading days to two years (Section 5.1.1); posterior draws are propagated through surrogate pricers for exotics, and Hellinger-SHAP attributes each parameter's prior-to-posterior contraction, a Kullback–Leibler or squared Hellinger divergence, to cells of the surface (Section 4). Section 3.1 describes how a posterior under a different prior, with support inside the training support, follows from the same ratio by reweighting with the ratio of prior densities (Eq. 3.7), and the paper says the uniform training prior lets alternative priors be imposed at inference time; a text search found no experiment applying one, every reported posterior using the training prior. Calibration of the parameter posteriors is checked by expected coverage under repeated simulation from the prior on a held-out set, after a post-training calibration of the classifiers (Section 5.2; Appendix E), and the price intervals against held-out Monte Carlo prices (Section 5.3.3); no exact or MCMC reference posterior is used and no Fisher information is computed. v1's sentence that neither Bayesian paper varies its prior stands on this reading as: neither runs an experiment that does, and the second describes the device. Of the four re-read, none measures a calibrator against an exact posterior, or under a prior other than the one it was trained on; for Hernandez, v1's record supports no more than that no uncertainty is attached.

**The pricer's foundations and the correlation range.** The characteristic function P5 prices from is El Euch and Rosenbaum (2016): Theorem 4.1 gives the semi-closed formula through the fractional Riccati equation, stated for a correlation ρ in (−1/√2, 1/√2]. The box under Objects runs ρ to −0.90, outside that interval. Abi Jaber, Larsson and Pulido (2019) prove the exponential-affine transform formula for the Volterra Heston model with ρ in [−1, 1] (Section 7, Eqs. 7.1 and 7.2, Theorem 7.1) and state that the fractional kernel recovers the rough Heston model and that the theorem generalises some of El Euch and Rosenbaum's main results (Example 7.2). On a reading of the two theorem statements, labelled as such, the formula the pricer implements is covered on the whole box by the second paper; the pricer's numerical behaviour on the box is what G0 measured.

**Identifiability of H from a surface.** What is on record: P1's finding on live Deribit surfaces that H rails to its bound as the flat direction of the Jacobian, degenerate with ν (D39, D41, D42, under What was known before freezing); Bayer, Horvath, Muguruza, Stemper and Tomas's unimodal posterior near the truth for rough Bergomi on one synthetic surface, along a ridge in η, H and ρ that it could not resolve (Section 5.3; Figure 6); Brigo, Huser and Leonte's attribution of each parameter's contraction to cells of the surface, under one prior (Section 4); and Hager and Kreher (2026), who state the fractional Riccati solution of the rough Heston model analytic in H, with low-order expansions in H accurate for European implied volatilities across many values of H (abstract): a statement about the smoothness of the map in H, which bears on how an emulator can represent it, not about its invertibility from quotes. None of these maps, on a stated box, grid and noise level, where H is identified and where it is not, with a measure of the exact posterior's contraction at each point. Novelty claim (i) is that map and no more.

**Bayesian prior-sensitivity analysis.** Gelman and co-authors (2020) set out the routes for assessing the influence of the prior: refitting under several priors, the most direct and, for a slow model, possibly prohibitive; comparing the prior's and the posterior's spread per parameter, the shrinkage stronger where the data are informative; importance sampling from one posterior to another when the two are close enough, a gap too large being informative in itself; and static sensitivity plots (Section 6.3). No rule is given for how far apart alternative priors should sit. Kallioinen, Paananen, Bürkner and Vehtari (2023) perturb one prior by raising it to a power near one, estimate the perturbed posterior by importance sampling, and measure sensitivity by the derivative with respect to the logarithm of the power of a cumulative Jensen–Shannon distance between the base and perturbed marginals, 0.05 being their threshold (Sections 2.4.2 to 2.4.4); they describe power-scaling as a local perturbation that may not change every quantity, and as a controlled way of comparing alternative priors (Sections 5.3 and 6). Elsemüller and co-authors (2024) fold the choice of prior and likelihood into the amortised network's conditioning, so that one network trained over a family of power-scaled priors returns the posterior under any member at inference time, and use deep ensembles to flag unreliable approximations on empirical data (Sections 3.1 to 3.3, 5.1 to 5.3); their validation is on simulated data with known truth, by error, calibration and contraction, with no reference posterior. P5 takes the first route on that list, with two priors fixed in advance and apart by a stated amount (D69), which none of the sources read prescribes; and what it asks is not whether the posterior moves with the prior, which Bayes' rule makes it along a flat direction, but whether a calibrator moves more than the exact posterior does.

**Unfaithful and misspecified amortised posteriors.** Hermans and co-authors (2022) report that every simulation-based inference algorithm they benchmark, amortised ratio estimation included, can return non-conservative posteriors, most at small simulation budgets; that deep ensembles raise expected coverage; and that an estimator returning the prior has expected coverage equal to the credibility level, so that coverage cannot measure what was learnt (Section 2.2; Observations 1 to 4 in Section 3.4). Their expected coverage is taken over the joint of the training prior and the simulator, and no shifted prior is evaluated. Delaunoy and co-authors (2022) add a balancing penalty to ratio estimation under which the approximation sits between the prior and the exact posterior in expectation, at a cost in information that a larger budget recovers and, for a strong penalty at a small budget, an approximation that reduces to the prior (Sections 3 and 4); conservativeness is again averaged over the training prior. Lueckmann and co-authors (2021) benchmark approximate posteriors against reference posteriors, drawn for the harder tasks by rejection sampling from a fitted proposal rather than taken from MCMC chains, two independent reference sets being checked indistinguishable by a classifier test (Appendix B.1), and compare by a classifier two-sample test, the choice of metric mattering (Sections 2.2 and 3); no task evaluates a posterior under a prior other than the training prior. Talts and co-authors (2018) validate posterior computation by the uniformity of rank statistics under the model's own prior and confine the check to the computation (Section 4; Algorithm 1). The misspecification strand concerns the data and the simulator more than the prior: Schmitt, Bürkner, Köthe and Radev (2022) flag at inference time, by a maximum mean discrepancy in a learned summary space, data the simulator and prior did not generate, a shifted or rescaled prior being among the cases detected, and report posterior error growing with that discrepancy (Sections 3.1 and 4); Cannon, Ward and Schmon (2022) move the observed data away from the simulator in five steps and find no tested mitigation, ensembles over seeds included, preventing failure in every case, the prior fixed throughout (Sections 3 to 5); Ward and co-authors (2022) model the discrepancy explicitly per summary statistic and name the statistics responsible, the prior fixed (Sections 2.1 and 2.2). The deep ensemble of mean-and-variance networks trained with the Gaussian negative log-likelihood and read as one Gaussian is Lakshminarayanan, Pritzel and Blundell (2017), presented as a non-Bayesian alternative with five members as the default (Sections 2.2 to 2.4; Algorithm 1). What these works test is faithfulness within the training prior, or robustness to data the simulator did not generate. P5 keeps the forward model well specified and shared, the calibrators and the reference facing the same emulated map and the same noise, shifts the prior by a stated amount, and measures each calibrator against an exact posterior computed per surface under the same prior, stratified by that posterior's contraction. Novelty claim (ii) is that measurement, in the calibration of a rough volatility model, and no more (D84).

**The novelty claim** is limited to (i) the identifiability map on a stated grid and (ii) calibrator fidelity measured against an exact posterior, stratified by contraction, in the calibration of a rough volatility model (D84). Claim (ii) carries a limitation (D81): verdicts on the full test set include extrapolation outside each network's training support, where the test θ lie far from its training prior's mass, and the secondary analysis on well-covered pairs (Analysis plan) limits that extrapolation. (i) is set against the posteriors of Bayer and co-authors and of Brigo, Huser and Leonte, each under one prior; (ii) against Hermans and co-authors' coverage within the training prior and Lueckmann and co-authors' benchmark against reference posteriors. Beyond the sources above, general work on simulation-based inference has begun to score amortised posteriors against reference posteriors under priors other than the training prior, and by the conditioning of the problem; this section does not survey that work, and claim (ii) does not extend to it (D84). Nothing else is claimed as new.
